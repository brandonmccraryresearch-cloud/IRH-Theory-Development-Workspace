# IX.3 — Requires External Structure: Research Brief

**Paper:** §IX.3. **Character:** problems whose solution, if it exists, comes
from outside the current framework — imported mechanisms, not derived ones. The
honest posture here is *interface specification*: state exactly what the
framework needs from the external structure, so that a candidate mechanism can
be checked against the requirement rather than hand-waved into place.

## 3.1 Lorentzian signature

**What the framework needs.** A mechanism by which a Euclidean-signature
substrate ($d=4$ base) yields Lorentzian-signature effective physics
($g_{00}=-1$ in the observed metric).

**Why it is hard.** The paper's diagnosis (§IX.3): published dynamical-signature
mechanisms (Mukohyama–Uzan [17], Kehayias–Mukohyama–Uzan [18]) require a
one-loop effective potential with **more fermionic than bosonic degrees of
freedom** — the Greensite condition [41], refined by Carlini–Greensite [42].
No purely classical Euclidean substrate is known to yield Lorentzian signature.
The v92 argument from $i^2=-1$ is invalid (retracted, Part VII): canonical
quantization uses complex amplitudes in Euclidean field theory too.

**Attack routes.**
- Survey the signature-change literature for any mechanism not requiring the
  fermion-dominance condition; if none exists, the requirement becomes a
  constraint on the framework's matter content (fermion DOF must exceed bosonic
  DOF at the scale where signature is fixed).
- Attempt the Mukohyama–Uzan construction concretely on the
  Swift–Hohenberg substrate: compute the one-loop effective potential for the
  $D_{4}$ background and check the sign structure. This is a real calculation with a
  yes/no answer.
- Note the coupling to reading (A) of the dimensional assignment (2.1): if 3.1
  is impossible in principle, reading (A) is dead and the decision collapses to
  (B) vs (C).

**Done looks like:** either a working signature mechanism adapted to the
substrate, or a no-go theorem stated with the same rigor as the SO(8) exclusion.

## 3.2 Einstein field equations

**What the framework needs.** Not effective curvature — *general relativity*:
$G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ with diffeomorphism invariance, the Bianchi
identity, universal coupling to conserved stress-energy, and two
transverse-traceless graviton polarizations.

**Why it is hard.** The paper's diagnosis (§IX.3): identifying the metric with
the substrate's local impedance tensor is the standard analog-gravity
construction [16] — wave propagation on a slowly varying background defines an
effective metric, and geodesic bending and horizons follow. But the effective
metric obeys the *substrate's* equation of motion, not Einstein's. This is a
general limitation of analog models (Sindoni [19]). "Condition-5-type reasoning
delivers effective curvature, **not general relativity**."

**Attack routes.**
- Map the precise gap: write the substrate's equation of motion for a
  slowly-varying $D_{4}$ background in geometric form, and identify exactly which
  of the GR structures (diffeomorphism invariance, Bianchi, universal coupling,
  TT polarizations) fail and where.
- Survey emergent-gravity programs (Sakharov induced gravity, Verlinde
  entropic gravity, thermodynamic derivations) for the *specific* missing
  structures; the question is not "is there an emergent-gravity literature" but
  "which of its mechanisms attach to a Swift–Hohenberg substrate."
- The graviton-polarization count is a sharp test: the substrate must yield
  exactly two TT modes, no more (extra polarizations are already constrained by
  gravitational-wave observations).

**Done looks like:** a derivation of the Einstein–Hilbert dynamics (or a
controlled approximation with stated error) from the substrate, or a precise
statement of which GR structures are unreachable and why.

## 3.3 Quantum mechanics beyond single-particle envelopes

**Status: a boundary, not a gap.** An envelope-limit Schrödinger equation is
established (§III.3, with quantified validity $a_0|\mathbf{k}| \lesssim 0.3$ for
1% accuracy). Reproducing entanglement and Bell-violating correlations from a
classical substrate requires either **nonlocality** or **superdeterminism**
(Hossenfelder–Palmer [38]).

**Working rule.** Do not treat this as a problem to be solved by more
substrate dynamics. Any extension of the framework into multi-particle quantum
phenomena must declare, up front, which horn of the dilemma it takes
(nonlocality or superdeterminism) and pay the associated price openly. The
paper's classification stands: this is where the framework ends, not where the
next calculation begins.

## 3.4 Monopole resolution

**The problem.** $E_{6} \to$ ⋯ $\to$ SU$(3)\times$SU$(2)\timesU(1)$ has $\pi_2(G/H)=\mathbb{Z}$: stable
magnetic monopoles are predicted (§V.3, §VI.7). Monopole *absence* is excluded;
the framework inherits the standard unified-theory monopole problem.

**Attack routes.**
- **Inflationary dilution** (standard): the framework needs an inflationary
  phase anyway if it is to address cosmological initial conditions (cf. 5.1,
  5.2). Check whether the Swift–Hohenberg substrate admits an inflation-like
  epoch — the first-order transition (§II.9) is suggestive but undeveloped.
- **Breaking pattern avoiding the topology:** classify $E_{6}$ breaking chains and
  identify any in which the unbroken subgroup avoids the $\pi_2$ obstruction
  at every stage. This couples to 4.1 (the $E_{6}$ breaking pattern must be worked
  out regardless).
- Note: this is a *prediction* (VI.7), not an embarrassment — but an
  undiluted monopole abundance contradicts observation, so the resolution is
  mandatory, not optional.

**Done looks like:** a stated resolution (dilution mechanism with numbers, or a
topology-avoiding breaking chain) consistent with the rest of the framework.
