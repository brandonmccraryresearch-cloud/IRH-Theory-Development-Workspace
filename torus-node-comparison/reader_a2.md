## (g) Internal node dynamics (§7): waves, rotor, resonance

**Role in the chain.** §7 studies the internal degrees of freedom of a node: waves on the flat torus, oscillations of its area, the rotor phase, and the windings. The section states upfront: "Except for Proposition 6.7, already stated in §6, these results are **not premises of the main argument**. Several statements concern explicit constructions: declared laws whose consequences are then proved. They are **proposed dynamics, not consequences of the axioms**." Figure 14 summarises: torus waves (helical plane-wave pair), breathing area mode (wave–geometry Hamiltonian), rotor and windings (phase transport), resonance and limits (exact/asymptotic retention).

**Waves (§7.1).** On the flat torus $T^{2}$_$\tau$ the plane waves $e^{2\pii(r\xi1+s\xi2)}$ have the frequencies of Def 6.6. A rotating (helical) plane wave is a superposition of modes (1,1) and (1,$-1)$ whose phases advance in time, so its pattern rotates around the torus. Prop 7.1: the modes have equal frequency iff |$\tau-1$| = |$\tau+1$|, i.e. $\mathrm{Re} \tau = 0$; on a rectangular torus $\tau$ = iy their superposition is a rigid rotation for every y; at fixed wave action the force the helical pair exerts on the shape vanishes iff y = 1 — the square torus is the shape equilibrium of a helical pair. The phase of a rotating wave advances linearly in time, so it "serves as a clock: it is the internal realisation of the unwound phase $\tilde{\phi}$ of Axiom I." The corresponding collective excitation is a scalar (acoustic) branch, not a spin-2 mode (No-go 7.8).

**Breathing and resonance (§7.2).** The breathing mode is the oscillation of the torus area A. By Prop 6.7 it couples to every wave mode with the same strength $-1/2$. The source is "in resonance" if the four cusp moves of a node carry equal energy, i.e. equal bath amplitudes $a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}$. Prop 7.2 (resonance makes spatial-move retention exact): if the bath is the breathing mode and the four cusp amplitudes are equal, the induced generator of the spatial-move-sequence dynamics is a multiple of the class sum of transpositions, $\Sigma_{\tau} L_{\tau}$; it commutes with every $R_{h}$, and the sequence defect vanishes exactly at every blocking step and every value of the regularisation. With unequal amplitudes a defect appears — at most **0.0095** for amplitudes (1, 0.96, 1.07, 1.02); numerically the defect is **$10^{-16}$** at all tested blocking steps (equal-amplitude case). Thm 6.9 shows the defect contracts to zero in the infrared. "Resonance is therefore 'exact if present, asymptotic otherwise'. **That resonance actually equalises the amplitudes is not derived.**" Parametric (Mathieu) resonance: breathing $A(t) = A_{0}(1+h \cos \Omegat)$ modulates mode frequencies, $\omega_{k}(t)^{2} = \omega_{k}^{2}(1 - h \cos \Omegat + O(h^{2}))$; each mode obeys a Mathieu equation; instability tongues, in particular $\Omega \approx 2\omega_{k}$, give exponential growth without a static tachyon — a candidate mechanism for dynamical formation of a condensate. **Not computed**: saturation by non-linear terms and constraints from conserved quantities remain; the mechanism remains **open**. Breathing-scalar mass: the area cost and wave zero-point energy produce the local minimum analysed in Prop 7.4; a sufficiently massive scalar has a Yukawa-suppressed force on Solar-System scales and can evade a long-range scalar constraint. Cassini measured PPN $\gamma [30]$; it did not measure this model's scalar mass. Compatibility requires both its mass and its coupling to matter, and **the source does not fix $\sigma$**.

**Common Hamiltonian (§7.3, Constr 7.3, Prop 7.4).** Wave–geometry Hamiltonian H (eq \$7.1$): q = (\rho, s, x)$$, area $$A = e^{\rho}$$, shape $$\tau = x$\$ + ie^s; two marked Fourier modes (0,1) and (1,0), each with two real quadratures $\chi$; $\omega_{1}^{2} = b^{2}e^{-\rho-s}$, $\omega_{2}^{2} = b^{2}e^{-\rho}(e^{s} + x^{2}e^{-s})$; M, $\sigma$, b, $\kappa > 0$; state is the ground state. Prop 7.4: (a) in finite volume H is self-adjoint with compact resolvent and a unique, strictly positive ground state; (b) single-node Born–Oppenheimer energy $E_{BO} = \sigmaA$ + bA^${-1/2}F(s,x)$ with $F = e^{-s/2} + \sqrt{(e^{s} + x^{2}e^{-s})} \geq 2$; unique minimum **$A* = (b/\sigma)^{2/3}**, s = x = 0 ($square torus), Hessian $\sigmaA*\cdot$diag(3/2, 1/2, 1) in $(\rho, s, x); (c)$ first wave–geometry vertex is cubic, $(1/2)\Sigma_{a} \partial$_$q \omega_{a}^{2}$|$\chi_{a}$|${}^{2}$; the breathing couples to both modes with the same sign, the shape s with opposite signs. Figure 15 (right) plots $E/(\sigmaA*) = a + 2/\sqrt{a}$ with a = A/A*; the caption warns the snapshot "does not demonstrate rotation of a superposition or dynamical equalisation of cusp amplitudes; resonance requires the separate assumptions in Proposition 7.2."

**Rotor (§7.4, Constr 7.5, Prop 7.6).** Wave–rotor Hamiltonian H* (eq 7.2): compact rotor $\phi_{i}$ at each node, coupling $g_{e}(q) = J\cdot\exp[-\nu(\rho_{y}-\rho_{x})^{2} - \mu\cdotd_{H}(\tau_{y},\tau_{x})^{2}]$, $r_{e}$ an external probe, $d_{H}$ the hyperbolic distance. **Reflection positivity vs sequence retention (§7.4).** A finite two-time construction with a Hermitian left-algebra generator passes all six listed checks (uniform ordered-sequence coefficients; [H, $R_{h}] = 0$ for all 24 h; reflection Gram matrix positive semidefinite; $M_{3}$ and $M_{2}$ blocks appear; static bath kernel $J_{ij}(0) = \varepsilon^{2}\Sigma_{b} g_{ib} g_{jb}/\Omega_{b}$ reproduced; finite positive partition function). **No-go results (§7.5) — the section's load-bearing content.**
- **No-go 7.7 (no chirality from rotation):** rotation of waves on a node torus cannot produce chiral zero modes. Dirac index on a closed surface depends only on the degree (total flux) of the connection (Atiyah–Singer [29]); rotation is momentum/holonomy, not flux; $\nabla\timesv$ has zero total flux by Stokes (torus has no boundary); total Gauss curvature is 0 by Gauss–Bonnet, so embedding curvature gives no net flux; counter-rotating branches are equally stable, so the sense of rotation is not selected.
- **No-go 7.8 (no graviton from torus oscillations):** area/shape oscillations do not produce a spin-2 field. Moduli $(\rho, \tau)$ are three real scalars per node (internal 2D metric up to diffeomorphism); a spacetime metric has ten components, of which a massless spin-2 field needs the two transverse-traceless ones built from spacetime indices; oscillations of internal scalars carry no spacetime tensor index; the only exact gauge direction is local rescaling; the collective mode is a scalar acoustic branch.
- **No-go 7.9 (no generations from hexagonal modes):** the threefold degeneracy of the lowest modes of the hexagonal torus $\tau = e^{i\pi/3}$ does not by itself give three chiral generations. With no background flux the torus Dirac operator anticommutes with chirality and non-zero eigenvalues occur in opposite-chirality pairs; the three lowest momenta form one orbit of the lattice rotation group — three equal non-zero Kaluza–Klein eigenvalues, not three unpaired zero modes; index contribution zero; the stabiliser permutes the three momenta, so geometry supplies no invariant rule retaining three chiral states while removing partners. A separate flux, boundary condition, or chiral projection would be additional input.
- **No-go 7.10 (no Higgs from breathing):** the breathing scalar cannot serve as the electroweak Higgs. $\delta\rho ($torus-area variation) is a gauge singlet (gauge transformations act on internal stack indices and leave $\rho$ unchanged); the stabiliser of a non-zero singlet VEV is the entire gauge group; EW breaking to $U(1)_{em}$ requires an SU(2) doublet with non-zero hypercharge; no potential for $\delta\rho$ alone can produce the required breaking.
- **No-go 7.11 (no spinors from triality):** triality of the $D_{4}$ cells does not endow a field placed on those cells with a spinor transformation law, and the displayed cell complex is not a single Kähler–Dirac complex. Triality permutes the three kinds of 16-cells, but in the present complex that permutation is realised by an element of $W(F_{4}) \subset O(4)$, so a full $2\pi$ geometric rotation acts trivially on cell labels; a spinor is a representation of the double cover Spin(4) on which the lifted $2\pi$ rotation acts as $-1$. Merely assigning a field to one of the triality orbits cannot change the former action into the latter. A Kähler–Dirac field in four dimensions has one component for every basis differential form, multiplicities (1, 4, 6, 4, 1), total dimension 16 (extracted text reads "total dimension 24 = 16", presumably $2^{4} = 16)$; the present incidence complex has cell multiplicities (1, 12, 32, 24, 3), totalling 72, and its incidence maps do not identify a 16-dimensional invariant subcomplex with those form degrees. **Axiom D remains an independent input.**
- **No-go 7.12 (failure of the tested partition-function combinations):** within the candidate family formed from the theta-sector sum, Haar gauge integral, Gaussian determinant, spatial-move-sequence path factor, and breathing bath, none of the explicitly tested combinations satisfies all six declared criteria simultaneously. Hermitian transfer $\Rightarrow$ reflected kernel is a Gram matrix (reflection positivity available) but the one-sided append operation is replaced by a symmetrised move and the ordered sequence is not retained; exact append generator $\Rightarrow$ ordered sequences retained but the generator is non-Hermitian and the reflected transfer has no positive Gram factorisation. Adding the theta sum, Haar integral, Gaussian determinant, or breathing kernel multiplies transfers by positive or central factors and does not alter this alternative. The two-time construction avoids this particular conflict by assigning the two operations to different parameters. Every member of the tested family uses a fixed graph rather than a fluctuating distance-defined graph, so Thm 5.1 cannot supply diffeomorphism invariance. A no-go for the stated candidate family, **not** for all possible partition functions.
