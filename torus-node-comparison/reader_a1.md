## (c) The D4 moves in the derivation chain (§3)

### How D4 selects 4 dimensions and one time direction
- The node position data are already 4 real numbers $(\tilde{\varphi}, \rho$, ℜ$\tau$, ℑ$\tau)$ — Thm 4.1 counts 1+1+2 = 4. D4 does not create the count; it **structures** it. The 24 index-2 Hurwitz relations, written in the D4 chart of Def. 3.5 (x = Hy, H the Hadamard-type matrix (3.8), $H^{-1} = H)$, are the roots $R = {\pm$ei $\pm$ ej} (3.6) with step log 2.
- Prop. 3.6: a root move changes log-area by $\Delta\rho = (\log 2)t(r)$, t(r) = ½$\Sigma$ri $\in {-1,0,1}$; $A' = 2^{t}(r) A$. Twelve difference roots (t=0) act in the 3D complement of the area axis; twelve sum roots $(t=\pm1)$ change area.
- Prop. 3.9 / Cor. 3.10: with the source weights w(r) = 2^$(-\betat(r))$, $\beta = 1 ($Def. 3.7 — an **input**, Remark 3.8), the weighted second-moment matrix is $\Sigmaw(r)$rrᵀ = $13P\perp + 15P0$ and the drift is $\Sigmaw(r)r = -(9/2)1$. Hence exactly two eigenvalues (13 on 1^$\perp, 15$ on R1) and drift along 1 only. One axis is distinguished by a different diffusion constant (ratio **15/13**) and by a drift; the other three are equivalent under S4 (irreducible 3D standard representation on 1^$\perp)$. Thm 4.3 identifies this distinguished axis with time and the drift with its arrow (orientation). Remark 3.11: the distinguished direction is the one along which t(r), i.e. log torus area, changes; generator = log det acting in the $\rho$ direction; conjugate clock reading = unwound phase $\tilde{\varphi}$.
- Time-adapted frame (Remark 3.11, eq. $3.11): H \in W(F4)$ = Aut(D4) sends ½1 to e0; in y = Hx the distinguished axis is y0, the twelve roots orthogonal to it are the cuboctahedron $\pm$ei$\pm$ej $(1 \leq i<j \leq 3)$, and the twelve roots with a time component are $\pme0\pm$ei. This frame is used for Lemma 4.10 (dispersion) and Thm 4.5 (reflection).

### The cuboctahedron spatial section
- §3.5: the twelve difference roots $\pm($ei$-$ej), length $\sqrt{2}$, lie in 1^$\perp$ and are the vertices of a cuboctahedron — the root polytope of A3, the spatial section of the 24-cell (whose 24 vertices are all D4 roots). ## (e) Gravity (§5)

- **Relational diffeomorphism invariance (Thm 5.1):** couplings depend only on geodesic distances and counts $\Rightarrow \Gamma[\psi$∗g] = $\Gamma[g]$ for quenched and annealed induced actions, for every orbifold diffeomorphism $\psi$. Proof via Lemma 3.3. This is the step the paper marks as derived (given the relational construction as declared).
- **No graviton mass (Cor. 5.2):** diffeomorphism Ward identity forbids $a p^{0}$ transverse-traceless term to all orders in h. Derived from Thm 5.1.
- **Fierz–Pauli form (Cor. 5.3):** conditional on the assumption that the leading non-zero term of the local quadratic expansion has two derivatives — then it is proportional to the linearised Einstein–Hilbert action. Fixed-crystal comparison (Num. $5.4): \Pi_{TT}(0) \approx 0.135$ on fixed D4/H4 crystal, Ward ratios fail — diagnostic of regulator symmetry breaking, not part of Cor. 5.3.
- **Induced action / Lovelock step (Thm 5.5):** the Einstein–Hilbert term arises as follows — (1) locality argued from exponential decay of matter propagators (Lemma 6.11, decay length $\approx 0.34\varepsilon$; paper: this "makes locality plausible but proves none of these continuum regularity statements"); (2) Thm 5.1 gives diffeomorphism invariance; count = volume fixes $\sqrt{g}$ so variations are traceless (unimodular); (3) the derivative-expansion hypothesis is ASSUMED: local diffeomorphism-covariant functional, leading Euler–Lagrange tensor natural/symmetric/divergence-free/linear in second derivatives, no unsuppressed nonlocal terms, torsion, extra light gravitational fields or >2-derivative terms (suppressed by $\varepsilon^{2}R)$; (4) Lovelock's theorem then gives aGµ$\nu$ + bgµ$\nu$; traceless variation + Bianchi + matter conservation $\Rightarrow G$µ$\nu + \Lambdag$µ$\nu = 8\piG T$µ$\nu (5.1)$ with $\Lambda$ an integration constant. The theorem does NOT determine the sign of G — that is the sign of c2 of the $p^{2}$ term. Numerical check (Num. $5.6): c2 \approx 1.0\times10^{-3} > 0$ in node units (attractive sign), exchange amplitudes agree with GR tensor ratios to $5\times10^{-15}$ — regulator checks, not part of the analytic implication.
