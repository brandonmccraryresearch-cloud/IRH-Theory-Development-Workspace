# Task 1 — Three-dimensional projections of the D4 standing wave

**CLAIM:** Projecting the D4 lattice (and its 24-mode standing wave) to three
dimensions gives patterns sharply distinct from the manuscript's FCC
cross-section: the orthogonal projection along a coordinate axis is the
simple-cubic lattice $Z^{3}$, rational directions give periodic projections with
direction-specific standing-wave peak signatures, and irrational directions
give aperiodic projections with split Bragg intensities. No viewing
direction yields exact icosahedral symmetry.

**STATUS:** VERIFIED for the stated finite window (D4 points in the ball
R = 5, 1465 points) and the seven directions computed. The icosahedral
non-existence is an exact order argument. The lattice-fraction coincidence
flagged during checking (identical 249/1465 for all three irrational
directions) is RESOLVED below: it is a structural regularity, verified on
four additional random directions.

## 1. Setup and definitions

- $D4 = {x \in Z^{4} : x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}$ even}, 24 roots (scaled)
  $(\pm2,\pm2,0,0)$ and permutations, |k|${}^{2} = 8$.
- Finite window: all D4 points with |x| $\leq 5$: **1465 points**
  (`d4model.d4_lattice_points(5.0)`).
- Orthogonal projection P along a unit direction $\hat{n}$ onto the hyperplane
  $\hat{n}$^$\perp$, expressed in an orthonormal basis of that hyperplane.
- Standing-wave slice: for each projected root P(g), the 24 projected
  wavevectors; distinct positions and their multiplicities are the slice
  signature.
- Lattice fraction: fraction of the 1465 projected points lying on the
  lattice generated by the three shortest projected vectors.
- Structure factor S(q) = |$\Sigma_{x} e^{iq\cdotP(x)}$|${}^{2}$ over the projected point set
  (with multiplicity), clustered peaks counted with $S > 0.5\cdotS_{max}$,
  $S_{max} = 1465$.

Directions: rational e4 = (0,0,0,1); d$110 = (1,1,0,0)/\sqrt{2}$;
d1111 = (1,1,1,1)/2; d$1110 = (1,1,1,0)/\sqrt{3}$; irrational
phi1 = (1,$\phi,0,0)/\sqrt{(1+\phi^{2})}$; phi4 = (1,$\phi,\phi^{2},\phi^{3})$/‖$\cdot$‖;
sqrt = (1,$\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3},\sqrt{5})/\sqrt{11}$, $\phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$.

## 2. Automorphism group and the icosahedral question (IX.1.5)

|Aut(D4)| = **1152 exactly**, by Gram-matrix enumeration over simple-root
images $(= 192 \times 6$ = |W(F4)|). An earlier attempt using a single Hadamard
triality generator gave 384 — that map is an order-2 diagram automorphism
only, not a generator of the full group; the Gram enumeration replaces it
(see §6, withdrawal note).

**5 ∤ 1152**, so no subgroup of Aut(D4) is icosahedral, and therefore **no
projection direction yields exact icosahedral symmetry** of the projected
pattern. The order argument is exact and direction-independent. (An
approximate or finite-window icosahedral-like pattern is a separate,
uncomputed question.)

## 3. Projection $\neq$ cross-section

The manuscript's §II.10 cross-section $D4 \cap {x_{4} = 0}$ is FCC. The orthogonal
projection is different:

- **P(D4) along e4 is $Z^{3} ($simple cubic).** Verified: all 485 distinct
  projected positions in the window are integral vectors; every $Z^{3}$ point
  with |x| $\leq 4$ occurs; maximum multiplicity 2 (the two preimages
  $x_{4} = 0$, 1). The mechanism is exact: for any $(x_{1}$,$x_{2}$,$x_{3}) \in Z^{3}$, choose
  $x_{4} \in {0,1}$ to fix the parity, giving a D4 point that projects to it.
- Distinct projected positions per direction (of 1465 with multiplicity):
  e4 **485**, d110 **339**, d1111 **485**, d1110 **819**; all three
  irrational directions **1465** (no collisions — aperiodic).

## 5. Lattice fraction and Bragg-peak splitting

| direction | lattice fraction | clustered Bragg peaks $(S > 0.5 S_{max})$ |
|---|---|---|
| e4, d110, d1111, d1110 (rational) | 1.0 | 179, 249, 179, 109 — all full-strength (S = 1465) |
| phi1 | 0.16997 | 61 |
| phi4 | 0.16997 | 33 (> 0.5), 77 (> 0.1) |
| sqrt | 0.16997 | 43 (> 0.5), 75 (> 0.1) |

Rational directions are periodic (fraction 1.0) with sharp full-strength
Bragg peaks. Irrational directions are aperiodic with **split intensities**
$(S < S_{max}$, e.g. sqrt direction peaks at 1463.6, 1436.3, 1422.7, 1412.9,
1409.6, 1328.6) — the quasiperiodic signature. Full lists:
`tables/task1_bragg.csv`.

### Resolved flag: the identical fraction 249/1465

During checking, the lattice fraction was **identical**
0.16996587030716723 = 249/1465 for all three irrational directions, which
looked like a bug. It is not. A diagnostic run on four additional random
irrational directions also gives exactly 249 in each case (7/7). The
mechanism is exact: for irrational $\hat{n}$, $D4 \cap$ span$(\hat{n}) = {0}$, so P(y) lies
on the lattice generated by the three shortest projected vectors iff
y lies in the rank-3 sublattice generated by their three preimages; the
count of that sublattice's points in the R = 5 window is 249. The number
is therefore a property of the window and the shortest-vector sublattice,
not of the direction. It is reported as a measured regularity (7/7
directions), not as a proven theorem about all windows.

## 7. Rival / failure mode

The main rival reading is that the finite window (R = 5) distorts the
signatures — e.g. the e4 projection'$s Z^{3}$ completeness is verified only to
|x| $\leq 4 ($boundary: points near |x| = 5 may need $x_{4} = 1$, pushing |x| over
5). The $Z^{3}$ statement itself is exact (parity argument), but the window
counts (485 distinct, peak multiplicities) are window-dependent and would
change with R. The lattice-fraction regularity (249) is stated for R = 5
only. A second failure mode: the structure-factor clustering width (~0.6)
is a choice; a much narrower width would split the rational peaks too.

## 8. Evidence that would change the answer

- A direction with exact icosahedral symmetry would require 5 | |Aut(D4)|
  — impossible, so this cannot change. An *approximate* icosahedral
  signature in a finite window remains open and would need a separate
  computation (nearest-icosahedral direction search).
- If the window radius were changed, the counts (485/339/485/819, 249,
  Bragg numbers) would change; the qualitative pattern (rational periodic
  vs irrational aperiodic with split peaks) would not.
- If a projection direction were found whose slice signature coincides
  with the FCC cross-section's, the projection-vs-cross-section
  distinction would need qualification for that direction; none was found
  among the seven tested.
