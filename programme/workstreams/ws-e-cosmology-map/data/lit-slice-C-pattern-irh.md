## §4 — Monopole dilution: what the literature quantifies (for ws-e)

Only source-quoted figures are given here; no numbers are asserted from memory.

- Preskill (1979) showed monopole–antimonopole annihilation after GUT-scale
  production leaves a relic density still far above the mass-density bound
  (DESY review summary: "still orders of magnitude too large"). A later
  thermal-production calculation (INSPIRE record, Phys. Lett. B 115 (1982)
  95–98) gives, for monopole mass / production temperature $m/T_{i} \approx 35$–55, a
  present monopole-to-photon ratio $n_{M}/n_{\gamma} \approx 10^{-23}$–$10^{-38}$ — i.e. the required
  suppression is exponential in $m/T_{i}$, and Preskill's Kibble-mechanism yield
  sits far above it.
- Turner (1984) states the glut most sharply: with Kibble production in
  standard cosmology, the universe would have reached 3 K at an age of
  ~10,000 years.
- Guth & Tye (1980) offer the non-inflationary route: suppression needs
  (i) the transition to occur after much supercooling and (ii) monopole mass
  large vs temperature immediately after — constraints on the transition, not
  a guaranteed outcome.
- Guth (1981) offers the inflationary route: exponential expansion dilutes
  any pre-inflationary relic density essentially to zero, at the cost of the
  graceful-exit problem (acknowledged in the paper).
- Relevance to IRH: IRH has a first-order transition (like Guth–Tye's
  supercooling route) but, as briefed, predicts no monopoles and states no
  dilution mechanism or relic abundance. The map should record this as an
  unaddressed comparison point, not as a failure: IRH does not currently
  compete on the monopole problem at all.

## §5 — Preliminary structural verdict (for ws-e synthesis; not a grade)

- **Genuine structural cousins (mechanism-level, not vocabulary):**
  Alexander–McTague (1978) and the Lifshitz–Petrich / Faraday-wave lineage
  (entries 7–13) share IRH's actual mechanism — selection among candidate
  patterns by counting zero-sum wavevector triads (cubic/3-body terms) on a
  shell of unstable modes. Wu–Plapp–Voorhees (2010) is the closest
  operational analogue (resonance tuning controls the selected symmetry).
  Brazovskii (1975) shares the shell + first-order-transition structure but
  selects stripes, not a lattice.
- **Ontological cousin, mechanistic stranger:** Volovik (2003) is the only
  entry sharing IRH's substrate ontology (physical substrate $\to$ emergent
  spacetime/effective metric), but its selection principle is Fermi-point
  topology, not resonance; and it shares IRH's effective-metric limitation.
- **Vocabulary-only lookalikes:** phase-field-crystal papers share the words
  "crystal", "lattice", "density field" and the Swift–Hohenberg mathematics,
  but their substrate is a material density field and their selection is
  ordinary free-energy minimisation over known crystal symmetries — no
  spacetime emergence, no D4, no cosmological prediction. Conway–Sloane
  supplies the D4 mathematics (densest 4D lattice packing, kissing number 24)
  but no dynamics at all. The E8/quasicrystal-tensor-network and
  vacuum-crystallisation items found in search (e.g. Quantum Gravity Research
  talks, 2026 Zenodo preprints on E8 vacuum lattices) were NOT tabled: they
  are non-peer-reviewed, share only the words "D4/E8/lattice/crystal", and
  state selection by postulate rather than by a dynamical criterion — the
  clearest vocabulary-only cases in this slice.
- **Two load-bearing caveats for ws-e:** (a) Klein (2001) argues
  Alexander–McTague solutions are unstable — IRH's stated 3D validation
  (recovering BCC) rests on a contested result and should be checked, not
  assumed; (b) IRH's selection principle changed between the SSRN predecessor
  (maximal cymatic complexity $\to$ dimension 8) and $v93.1 (S = \tau^{2}/q_{4} \to D4)$ —
  the map should note which principle is being compared.
- **The gap no cousin closes:** no pattern-formation entry combines a
  physical substrate with a triad-resonance lattice selection AND a
  quantitative cosmological prediction. The triad-selection literature is
  quantitatively predictive only at lab/materials scales; the substrate
  literature (Volovik) is not lattice-selecting; IRH is the only entry
