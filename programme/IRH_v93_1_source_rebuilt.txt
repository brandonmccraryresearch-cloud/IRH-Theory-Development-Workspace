the Higgs vacuum expectation value, 𝐴3 resonance mass and widths, and CKM mixing. The
gauge decision makes a route available; it does not rehabilitate the prior numbers, which were
computed in an excluded framework and carried independent arithmetic errors. Nothing from
those calculations should be carried forward.


IX.5 The Genuinely Open Physical Questions

Two questions are sharper than the rest and would most advance the programme.

                                              29
What fixes 𝜓̄ ? Under conserved dynamics it is an initial datum rather than a tuned coupling,
which reclassifies the fine-tuning objection as a cosmological initial-condition question — a
different and weaker charge. But no mechanism sets its value, so whether occupying the D₄
band is a prediction or a tuning remains undetermined.
What supplies the nucleation event? The transition is first order (§II.9), so the D₄ phase must
be nucleated. Spontaneous emergence from a featureless substrate does not occur at the
relevant baseline tension.



PART X — ASSESSMENT

Five verification cycles have produced a framework materially different from the one they be-
gan with, and smaller. Those facts are related.
What has been removed was in every case removed for cause: an anisotropy coefficient not
derivable from the stated lattice; a gauge algebra excluded by a Weyl-group computation; time-
step values violating their own stability bound; a generation count resting on a conflation of
symmetry with multiplicity; a claimed absence of monopoles inconsistent with the adopted
gauge group. What remains has been computed, validated against known limiting cases, sim-
ulated where simulation was possible, and in one instance corroborated by an independent
published result obtained by an unrelated method.
The reformulation from static geometry to interference dynamics was the decisive move. It
removed a primitive, dissolved a standing objection at its root, supplied the selection mecha-
nism the static formulation never had, and produced falsifiable predictions the static version
could not state. That reformulation is not a reinterpretation of the same theory. It is a different
and better one.
The honest position. IRH is a well-posed research programme with a verified geometric core,
a defensible gauge identification, a quantified stability domain, and a specific list of open
problems. It is not a completed unified theory, and the distance to one is large — measured
precisely by Part IX. Its central claim, that a high-symmetry discrete structure emerges dynami-
cally from an oscillatory substrate rather than being imposed, is at this point supported rather
than asserted. That is a smaller claim than v92 made, and it is one that has survived adversarial
checking.



Appendix A: Complete Record of Corrections

Errors found across five verification cycles, including in this author’s own output. Each is
recorded because each altered a conclusion.

   1. Anisotropy verification. An audit document’s claimed confirmation of 𝜉2 = −1/384 did
      not survive recomputation: the D₄ fourth moment is exactly isotropic, and the document’s
      own substitution yields −1/768.

  2. Real-space comparison methodology. An initial wavepacket test produced erratic 30–
     200% errors, traced to a phase-sensitive metric and excessive momentum spread rather
     than to the derivation.

  3. Robustness scan. A scan suggesting A₄* beats D₄ applied a near-threshold criterion out-
     side its regime and double-counted D₄ against its own dual.

                                                30
  4. Experiment design. A simulation intended to place two shells in degeneracy placed the
     resonant wavenumber on a third shell. The run proved informative: that shell had zero
     triad content and produced lamellae, confirming the mechanism negatively.
  5. Incomplete free-energy comparison. Sub-patterns within a shell were omitted; simula-
     tion exposed the omission, leading to the identification of baseline tension as control
     parameter.
  6. Conjugacy classes of 2T. Trace and element order merge distinct classes, giving five in-
     stead of seven; corrected by conjugation-orbit computation.
   7. Weight classes in 𝑃/𝑄. An initial computation returned class 0 for all six fundamental
      weights, inconsistent with a nontrivial centre; corrected via Smith normal form.
  8. Mixed normalization. A free-energy scan combined normalized 𝜏, 𝑞4 with a raw mode
     count, giving 63.8% against the correct 11.9%.

  9. Explicit integration instability. An explicit Euler step against an operator reaching 6×104
     overflowed to NaN.
 10. Conclusion contradicted by data. A script printed the pre-written conclusion ”Model B
     preserves the D₄ state” while its own output showed the opposite. The correct expla-
     nation — a sub-critical seed at a first-order transition — was found only by rejecting the
     printed text.
  11. Spectrum normalization. Power fractions computed without subtracting the mean, so
      nonzero 𝜓̄ dominated the 𝑘 = 0 bin.
 12. Seed amplitude normalization. Seeds normalized by peak rather than per-mode ampli-
     tude, suppressing effective amplitude by a factor of 24.

 13. Timestep in conserved dynamics. The explicit nonlinear term is multiplied by 𝑘2 reaching
     256; 𝑑𝑡 = 0.2 diverged.
 14. Conflation of tuned and detuned 𝑞0 . The phase diagram used bare 𝑟 while simulations
     used detuned 𝑞0 ; reconciling them produced the qualification of §II.7.
 15. Unstated domain convention. The simulation box length and shell normalization were
     never stated, making the numerics non-reproducible. Identified in independent review;
     fixed in §II.6.
 16. Unstated free-energy convention. 𝐹 as excess-over-uniform was not declared, producing
     an apparent 1.2% discrepancy in independent review that turned out to be harmonic
     content (§II.8).
  17. Unspecified competitor shell. The key dynamical selection test in §II.6 could not be re-

      dent review; now fully specified.
 18. Unstated table convention. The A₄/A₄* rows are labelled by crystal while showing

     added.

Items 1, 3, 5, 8, 10, 12 and 14 each changed a reported conclusion. Items 15–18 were repro-
ducibility defects rather than errors of result: in each case the computation was right and the
reporting was incomplete. They are recorded at the same level of prominence because a result
that cannot be reproduced from the text is not, for a reader, distinguishable from one that is
wrong.

Appendix B: Computational Methods

Symbolic computation used SymPy [36] throughout; the Euler–Lagrange variation, dispersion
reduction, ordered-branch solution, and effective-coefficient expansion were all obtained
symbolically rather than by hand. Lie-algebra results were cross-checked in Wolfram Language
by generating Weyl groups from simple reflections, an algorithm structurally independent
of the signed-permutation construction used in Python. Numerical optimization used SciPy
[11] (L-BFGS-B and differential evolution). Higher-order mode counting used FFT convolution
on a 324 grid, validated against brute-force enumeration at third and fourth order. Field
simulations used a semi-implicit pseudo-spectral scheme [10] on a 164 periodic grid, with
the linear operator treated implicitly in Fourier space and timesteps selected separately
for conserved and non-conserved dynamics according to each scheme’s stability constraint.
Phase-diagram computation involved order 104 independent free-energy minimizations per
parameter setting. Group-theoretic results for 2T were obtained by exhaustive enumeration:
the complete product table, conjugation orbits over all elements, and explicit coset represen-
tatives. Random seeds were fixed for reproducibility. All figures were generated directly from
the computations reported, with no illustrative or placeholder data.


Appendix C: Terminology

This document uses standard mathematical and physical terminology — Lie algebra, gauge
field, discrete Laplacian, dispersion relation, order parameter — rather than framework-
internal relabellings of the same objects. This is deliberate. The results above are useful only
to the extent that they can be checked against the existing literature on spherical designs, Lie
algebra classification, and pattern formation, and renaming standard objects would obstruct
exactly that.
