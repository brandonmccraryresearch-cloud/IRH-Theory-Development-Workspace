# Full-field linearization of the D₄ standing-wave ansatz

## Result and scope

**The frozen, unrelaxed 24-mode ansatz has four positive finite-box Model B eigenvalues, each approximately +0.0228772435.** Their right eigenvectors are translation-like: 99.996124% of their Fourier power is on the occupied star. Their small harmonic tails lift the four zero directions of the retained-star calculation. The other 31 integer momentum cosets have negative leading rates. The leading separate harmonic-dominated eigenmode has rate −1027.44544.

**This is not a stability test of the harmonically relaxed equilibrium.** The supplied ansatz is stationary only after projection onto its 24 occupied modes. Its full-field Model B residual has Fourier L² norm 4.25853893, entirely off the occupied star to numerical precision. Thus the calculation is an instantaneous Jacobian and energy-curvature calculation at a nonstationary field. Positive frozen rates do not establish exponential departure from a nearby relaxed standing wave.

The calculation covers all integer Fourier wavevectors in six reciprocal cubes on the box [0,2π]⁴, up to 83,520 mean-zero Fourier degrees of freedom. It includes harmonics and the neighboring integer shells. **No continuous Bloch scan or larger-box limit is performed.** The momentum figure is a discrete, symmetry-reduced map, not a continuous instability band.

## 1. Functional, base field, and operator

Sources are the supplied v93.1 manuscript, §§I.2–I.3 and II.7–II.9 (especially pp. 4 and 9–11), and the three supplied basin-study files. The manuscript explicitly distinguishes its one-shell ansatz from its harmonically relaxed field; that distinction is retained here. All four inputs are SHA-256 recorded in the numerical archive.

The functional and conserved dynamics are

\[
F[\psi]=\int d^4x\left[\frac12\psi\{r+(q_0^2+\nabla^2)^2\}\psi-\frac u3\psi^3+\frac v4\psi^4\right],\qquad
\partial_t\psi=\nabla^2\mu,\quad
\mu=\{r+(q_0^2+\nabla^2)^2\}\psi-u\psi^2+v\psi^3.
\]

Parameters are r=−0.35, mean m=−0.18, q₀²=8, u=v=1, and mobility 1. Frequencies are physical integer wavevectors k=n, not unscaled roots. The base is

\[
\psi_0(x)=m+A\sum_{s\in S}e^{is\cdot x},\quad
S=\{2(\pm e_i\pm e_j):i<j\},\quad
A=0.07757529633928076.
\]

Here α=r−2um+3vm²=0.1072, β=u−3vm=1.54, and A is the larger root of α−8βA+141vA²=0. No amplitude rounding or harmonic relaxation is applied.

For ψ=ψ₀+η, the exact first-order expansion is

\[
\partial_t\eta=R_0+L\eta+O(\eta^2),\qquad
R_0=\nabla^2\mu[\psi_0],\qquad
L=\nabla^2 H,
\]
\[
H=r+(q_0^2+\nabla^2)^2-2u\psi_0+3v\psi_0^2.
\]

With Fourier coefficients a₀=m and aₛ=A, define Wₚ=−2uaₚ+3v∑ₛaₛaₚ₋ₛ. On the finite set G_K={k∈ℤ⁴: |kᵢ|≤K} with k=0 removed,

\[
H_{k\ell}=\{r+(8-|k|^2)^2\}\delta_{k\ell}+W_{k-\ell},\qquad
L_{k\ell}=-|k|^2 H_{k\ell}.
\]

Polynomial convolutions are evaluated by exact integer index sums. There is **no FFT wrapping or aliasing**. Couplings with either endpoint outside G_K are discarded by Galerkin projection. In particular, the small K=3 calculation need not retain every nonzero Fourier coefficient of W or of R₀; coefficients are still constructed before projection.

Although L is not symmetric, with D=diag(|k|²)>0 it is similar to

\[
B=-D^{1/2}HD^{1/2},\qquad L=D^{1/2}BD^{-1/2}.
\]

The code solves the real symmetric B problem, then saves physical **right** eigenvectors h=D¹ᐟ²y normalized by ∑ₖ|hₖ|²=1. All table power fractions refer to h, not y. Rates have inverse dimensionless Model B time units.

### Mean conservation

The k=0 coefficient of every perturbation is fixed to zero. All occupied-star, harmonic, and sideband channels reported here are mean-conserving. No unconstrained mean-shift Hessian direction is mixed into these results, and no trivial mass-conservation zero eigenvalue is included. If the zero coefficient were retained as a variable, its Model B equation would be identically zero; it would not supply a growing mean channel.

## 2. Momentum and symmetry blocks

Every potential shift lies in the lattice generated by the occupied star,

\[
\Lambda=2D_4=\{2n:n\in\mathbb Z^4,\ \sum_i n_i\ \text{even}\}.
\]

Therefore Fourier indices only couple within a coset of ℤ⁴/Λ. There are 32 exact momentum blocks. A convenient label is

\[
p_i=k_i\bmod2,\qquad t=\tfrac12(\sum_i k_i-\sum_i p_i)\bmod2.
\]

Signed coordinate permutations preserve both the base and the reciprocal cube. They reduce the 32 cosets to six representative calculations. No unrestricted triality symmetry of the cubic cutoff is assumed.

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D4 | (0,0,0,0;0) | 1 | 3,280 | **+0.0228772435** |
| even_other | (0,0,0,0;1) | 1 | 3,280 | −64.3152700919 |
| odd1 | (0,0,0,1;0) | 8 | 2,916 | −11.2130362987 |
| odd2 | (0,0,1,1;0) | 12 | 2,592 | −25.3674295892 |
| odd3 | (0,1,1,1;0) | 8 | 2,304 | −7.2696692890 |
| odd4 | (1,1,1,1;0) | 2 | 2,048 | −64.3152700921 |

The multiplicity-weighted dimensions sum to 83,520=(17⁴−1). Forty leading eigenpairs are saved per representative and cutoff, giving 1,440 table rows and 240 K=8 eigenpairs. These are partial spectra, not all 83,520 eigenvectors. Within self-inverse cosets, degenerate eigenspaces are resolved by inversion parity. Odd1 and odd3 require conjugate partner cosets to form real fields. Other point-group irreducible representations are not assigned.

## 3. Mode identities

The leading modes in the D4 block separate into the following rate groups at K=8. Multiplicities refer to this block and are counted within the computed spectrum.

| Rate | Multiplicity | Main identity |
|---:|---:|---|
| +0.0228772435 | 4 | Odd, translation-like occupied-star modes with harmonic tails |
| −2.7155295372 | 9 | Even occupied-star distortions |
| −5.9053489219 | 1 | Even occupied-star distortion |
| −6.2575877388 | 8 | Odd occupied-star distortions |
| −11.5204687529 | 2 | Even occupied-star distortions |
| −1027.44544384 | 2 | First separate harmonic-dominated group, mainly q²=16 |

The positive quartet has occupied-star power 0.999961239939, quadratic-generated harmonic power 0.000038511383, and remaining power about 2.487×10⁻⁷. Its overlap power with span{ikⱼaₖ : j=1,…,4}, the infinitesimal translation subspace, is 0.999961239939. The leading harmonic group is 0.999999825760 on the quadratic-generated harmonic set and has negligible occupied-star weight.

The nearest-shell sideband identities are:

- **odd3** — leading rate −7.2696692890; 99.997422% of right-mode power is on |k|²=7, the manuscript’s competitor shell.
- **odd1** — leading rate −11.2130362987; 99.849112% is on the neighboring |k|²=9 shell.
- **odd2** — leading modes are dominated by |k|²=6; their leading rate is −25.3674295892.
- **even_other and odd4** — leading modes are dominated by |k|²=4, with rates near −64.31527009. Their near-equality at large cutoff is observed numerically, not used to reduce the six calculations to fewer blocks.

“Harmonic” power in the CSV has a precise diagnostic meaning: (S+S)\(S∪{0}). The operator itself includes all available higher harmonics, including those outside this first generated set. “Near-shell sideband” power means |k|²=7 or 9 outside the occupied star and harmonic set. Other off-shell weights are recorded separately. On this integer grid the complete |k|²=8 shell is exactly the 24 occupied wavevectors; there are no additional unoccupied integer vectors at the same radius.

Individual eigenvectors inside degenerate groups are not unique. Dominant-k entries and plotted slices illustrate a numerical basis, not a preferred physical direction. Compare degenerate subspaces, rates, or shell-power identities rather than signs of individual coefficients.

## 4. Why the four phase zeros become positive rates

The full residual has 600 nonzero signed off-star Fourier coefficients. Its main shell contributions are:

| Squared radius | Residual modes | ‖μₖ‖₂ | ‖R₀,ₖ‖₂ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 16 | 24 | 0.10774319 | 1.72389101 |
| 24 | 96 | 0.03794968 | 0.91079236 |
| 32 | 24 | 0.00948742 | 0.30359745 |
| 40 | 144 | 0.08403164 | 3.36126556 |
| 48 | 96 | 0.02744462 | 1.31734168 |
| 56 | 192 | 0.01940628 | 1.08675144 |
| 72 | 24 | 0.00228705 | 0.16466771 |

The occupied-star RHS norm is 2.22×10⁻¹⁶; the full RHS norm is 4.25853893. Norms use Parseval density normalization, ∑|cₖ|²=(1/V)∫|c(x)|²dx, not the volume-multiplied integral. All residual support is contained by K=6. A large residual derivative norm need not imply a large eventual harmonic amplitude: the harmonic channels have very large damping rates. This calculation does not integrate that relaxation or reproduce the manuscript’s relaxed energy.

Translation covariance gives

\[
L\,\partial_j\psi_0=\partial_jR_0,
\]

not zero at this ansatz. The identity is numerically satisfied to 3.1×10⁻¹⁶. Hence symmetry does not require four exact Goldstone zeros at this nonstationary field. Exact translation zeros would instead be required about a stationary, harmonically relaxed periodic solution with nontrivial dependence on all four coordinates.

A second check explains the curvature sign. Partition the D4 block into occupied-star coordinates S and off-star coordinates Q. The Q block is positive definite, and the static harmonic-eliminated Hessian is

\[
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H_{SS}-H_{SQ}H_{QQ}^{-1}H_{QS}.
\]

At K=8 it has **4 negative, 0 zero, and 20 positive** eigenvalues, with minimum −0.00285971043. In contrast, HSS has 0 negative, 4 zero, and 20 positive eigenvalues. Sylvester inertia then gives four negative directions in the full finite-box Hessian and four positive Model B rates. This is an independent count, not a count inferred only from the first few Ritz values. The Schur eigenvalues are static Hessian curvatures; they must not be multiplied by a common mobility 8 to obtain the full-field rates.

There is also a useful analytic bound specific to this integer box. Since −2uψ+3vψ²≥−u²/(3v)=−1/3, any nonoccupied integer Fourier subspace has Hessian lower bound

\[
H\ge\min_{k\notin S,\ k\ne0}\{(8-|k|^2)^2-0.35-1/3\}\ge0.3166667.
\]

Within the D4 harmonic complement its sharper lower bound is 63.3166667, since the next allowed squared radius is 16. Thus purely off-star directions have positive curvature; the small negative directions arise through coupling to the phase-null occupied-star subspace. The other 31 cosets do not couple to the occupied star and are positive definite. These bounds depend on integer-shell spacing and **do not rule out fractional Bloch perturbations near the resonant sphere**.

## 5. Cutoff convergence

Cutoffs are coordinate cubes, not radial shells. Successive odd/even cutoffs can leave an all-even block unchanged; repeated entries are therefore expected, not duplicated experiments.

| K | Mean-zero grid dimension | D4 leading rate | odd3 leading rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 2,400 | +0.0132390571 | −7.2742436125 |
| 4 | 6,560 | +0.0220695840 | −7.2710418030 |
| 5 | 14,640 | +0.0220695840 | −7.2701412341 |
| 6 | 28,560 | +0.0228772430 | −7.2696692911 |
| 7 | 50,624 | +0.0228772430 | −7.2696692891 |
| 8 | 83,520 | +0.0228772435 | −7.2696692890 |

Absolute changes of each block’s largest rate between K=6 and K=8 are 5.46×10⁻¹⁰ (D4), 2.08×10⁻⁹ (even_other), 1.07×10⁻⁸ (odd1), 1.12×10⁻⁹ (odd2), 2.12×10⁻⁹ (odd3), and 2.42×10⁻⁸ (odd4). Quoted leading-rate convergence is therefore roughly 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁷ absolute, not a certified infinite-cutoff error bound. The D4 change is already comparable with numerical residual scales; do not read all printed digits as independently converged.

Convergence is slower farther down the spectra: the maximum rank-matched change among all first 40 eigenvalues is about 5.67×10⁻⁴ between K=6 and K=8. The archive keeps all 1,440 rates so this distinction is inspectable.

Eigenpairs are found by symmetric shift-invert at σ=6, which selects the largest eigenvalues rather than merely eigenvalues near zero. This is justified by the global integer-grid upper bound

\[
\lambda_{\max}(B)\le\max_{n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}}n\{0.35+1/3-(8-n)^2\}=5.4666667<6.
\]

Dense diagonalization is used for blocks smaller than 50 or too small for the requested sparse solve.

## 6. Retained-star reference and numerical checks

The 24×24 Hessian is recomputed analytically from the same polynomial functional, with orthonormal Fourier/real-field normalization. The six- and twelve-mode amplitudes are independently obtained from their restricted stationarity equations; no trajectory endpoints are required.

| State | Negative / zero / positive Hessian directions | Minimum Hessian eigenvalue | Largest retained Model B rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Uniform | 0 / 0 / 24 | +0.1072 | −0.8576 |
| Full24 | 0 / 4 / 20 | ≈0 | ≈0 |
| Exact six-mode | 6 / 2 / 16 | −0.1072 | +0.8576 |
| Exact twelve-mode | 2 / 3 / 19 | −0.02586715184 | +0.20693721471 |

All signs reproduce the supplied `stability_checks.csv`. Maximum discrepancy in the four minimum eigenvalues is 1.51×10⁻¹². The full24 smallest positive retained Hessian eigenvalue is +0.34189913748. Only in this single-radius reference is the conversion λ=−8h valid for every mode.

Additional validation:

- Centered finite difference of the full polynomial chemical potential versus analytic Hη: relative L² error 1.10×10⁻¹².
- Hessian symmetry error: zero in the tested block.
- Dense versus production eigensolver cross-check: maximum eigenvalue discrepancy 4.68×10⁻¹³.
- Independent leading spectra of all 32 K=3 cosets versus the six representatives: maximum difference 7.76×10⁻¹².
- Maximum absolute residual ‖By−λy‖₂ over all saved pairs: 5.23×10⁻⁹. Maximum normalized right-mode residual ‖Lh−λh‖₂: 1.90×10⁻⁸.
- The multiplicity-weighted block dimensions reproduce the complete mean-zero grid dimension at every cutoff.

These are floating-point validation checks, not interval-certified spectral enclosures.

The executable source is also kept at `co-scientist-outputs/d4-offshell-study/run_offshell.py`. From the workspace root:

```bash
python3 co-scientist-outputs/d4-offshell-study/run_offshell.py \
  --out co-scientist-outputs/d4-offshell-reproduced
```

Defaults are `--cutoffs 3 4 5 6 7 8 --nev 40`. `--out` sets the output directory, `--cutoffs` selects reciprocal coordinate bounds (integers ≥2), and `--nev` sets eigenpairs per representative, capped by block dimension. Smaller values of `--nev` may omit the first harmonic-dominated group. The script checks the supplied basin reference when that original path is available, but the numerical computation does not depend on it or on manuscript access.

After extracting the ZIP elsewhere, run `python3 run_offshell.py --out reproduced`. Dependencies are Python ≥3.10, NumPy, SciPy, and Matplotlib; exact versions used here are recorded in `parameters_validation.json`. The report is a written interpretation of this run, not generated by the numerical script.

Auxiliary archive entries include `growth_modes.csv` (all cutoffs), `retained_reference.csv`, `retained_spectrum.csv`, `schur_spectrum.csv`, `ansatz_residual.csv`, `residual_shells.csv`, `potential_coefficients.csv`, six `operator_H_*.npz` sparse matrices at K=8, and `parameters_validation.json` with source/input hashes and validation metrics. For a saved block, recover its full physical Jacobian as `L = -sparse.diags((K*K).sum(axis=1)) @ H`. Only H and the representative wavevectors are needed; no dense full-grid matrix is necessary.

For a self-inverse block, reconstruct an even real mode directly from its real coefficients and an odd real mode from i times those coefficients. For a paired coset, combine h on the representative and its conjugate on −k with factors 1/√2. Signed coordinate permutations supply the remaining symmetry-related cosets. These conventions give real perturbations of unit Fourier L² norm; plotted transverse slices use x₃=0.371 and x₄=0.619 to avoid special nodal planes.

## Interpretation for the next full-field experiment

The immediate response of an exact one-star seed includes deterministic harmonic forcing, even with no added perturbation. That must be separated from growth of perturbations. The positive frozen quartet is phase-like and does not identify a new competing integer shell. The nearest integer sidebands at squared radii 7 and 9 are damped at this point.

A stability claim about the manuscript’s relaxed standing wave requires first solving for a mean-constrained harmonic equilibrium and bounding its residual, then recomputing its spectrum. A claim beyond this particular box additionally requires Bloch offsets or larger boxes, including long-wave mass and displacement channels. Neither step is replaced by reciprocal-cutoff convergence here. This study establishes the finite-box operator, its channel identities, and the nonstationary ansatz’s spectrum; it does not establish stability or instability to all Bloch perturbations, nucleation behavior, or unrestricted dynamical D₄ selection.
