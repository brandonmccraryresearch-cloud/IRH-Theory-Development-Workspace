## 2. SO(8) exclusion, chirality leg — REFINED (conclusion stands, one intermediate claim overstated)

The Lean module `lean/Irh/WeylD4.lean` proves exactly what it claims: an
explicit 12-reflection word equals $-I$, so $-I \in W(D_4)$. The inference
"$-1 \in W \Rightarrow$ every irrep self-conjugate" is a standard theorem. Both
are solid.

The external audit's logical point is **correct**: self-conjugate does not imply
real — a self-conjugate irrep may be pseudoreal (quaternionic); the SU(2)
doublet is the canonical example. So the manuscript's intermediate sentence
("The three eight-dimensional representations are all real", "An algebra with
only real representations is vector-like") claims more than $-1 \in W(D_4)$
alone establishes. The 8v/8s/8c reality is true for $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ but is an
additional fact, not a consequence of the Weyl-group computation.

**However**, the audit's framing understates how much follows: the physical
conclusion — "$\mathfrak{so}(8)$ admits no chiral gauge theory" — follows from
**self-conjugacy of all irreps alone**, without any real/pseudoreal
classification. A 4D gauge theory is chiral iff some Weyl fermion sits in a
complex representation (inequivalent to its conjugate). If every irrep is
self-conjugate, no complex irreps exist, and every left-handed fermion pairs
with its right-handed conjugate: the theory is vector-like. Pseudoreal reps do
not rescue chirality (cf. SU(2) gauge theory, which is vector-like for any
fermion content). So:

- The **conclusion** (no chiral $\mathfrak{so}(8)$ gauge theory) is defensible
  from the formalized fact + standard representation theory.
- The **exposition** should be weakened: replace "only real representations"
  with "every representation self-conjugate, hence none complex". The
  real/pseudoreal split is not needed for the no-go and is not established by
  the cited computation.

The second leg (Borel–de Siebenthal maximal-rank embedding) is prose-level in
the manuscript, cited to the literature, and is **not** represented in the Lean
modules or the 51 Python checks. That is accurately reported by the audit; it is
a formalization gap, not evidence of error.

**Workspace action:** `guardrails/EXCLUDED.md` updated to record the refined
inference chain (see commit).

## 3. CFL stability table — CONFIRMED (labeling error; verdicts correct)

The manuscript's table (§III.2) heads the column "$\sin^2(\omega\Delta t/2)$ at
worst-case $k$" and lists $4/3 \approx 1.333$ and $16\pi^2/3 \approx 52.6$.
A sine-squared cannot exceed 1; these are values of the dimensionless stability
parameter $P = \frac{4}{3}(c\Delta t/a_0)^2$ evaluated before rejection. The
instability verdicts (unstable / badly unstable / stable) are consistent with
$P \le 1 \iff \Delta t \le \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a_0/c$.

**Status:** manuscript erratum — rename the column to the stability parameter
$P$ and state $P \le 1$. Conclusions unaffected.

## 4. Resonance maxima 16 vs 32 — RESOLVED (audit's "unresolved" superseded by direct proof)

Executed `verification/reconcile_resonance_count.py` (exit 0). Exhaustive
enumeration on the paper's exact §II.6 physical grid — $16^4$ points, box
$L = 2\pi$, scaled $D_4$ shell $\{(\pm2,\pm2,0,0)\}$ with $|k|^2 = 8$ — of
$P(x) = \sum_{k} \cos(k \cdot x)$:

- Global maximum 24 attained at **exactly 32 grid points**.
- Analytic characterization confirmed point-for-point: maxima $\iff$
  $x = (\pi/2)m$ with all $m_i$ of the same parity — **16 corners** ($m$ all
  even) **+ 16 body-centres** ($m$ all odd). Enumerated set equals analytic
  prediction exactly.
- Body-centre translation $t = (\pi/2)(1,1,1,1)$ is a symmetry of $P$ to
  $3 \times 10^{-14}$.

The manuscript's corner/body-centre explanation is **correct**. The shipped
verifier's 16-count is also correct — for its own setup (closed fundamental
cell, unscaled dual shell), where the 16 corners are one maximum modulo $D_4$
periodicity. The two counts are different grids, not a contradiction.

**Presentation caveat (new):** the 32-count belongs to the *scaled-shell
physical pattern* of §II.6, but §II.2's scope paragraph states it in the
context of the *unscaled* $\Psi$ scan. On the same $L = 2\pi$ grid the unscaled
$\Psi$ has maxima at $D_4$ points, which (by irrationality of $\pi$) essentially
never coincide with grid points — so the paragraph as placed reads as if the
32 were a property of the just-scanned $\Psi$. The count is right; its placement
invites misreading. Noted for manuscript revision.

## 5. Envelope error table — CONFIRMED (under-specified)

The manuscript's table (§III.3) lists relative frequency errors at
$a_0|k| \in \{0.02, 0.10, 0.30, 1.00, \pi\}$ but does not specify the carrier
angle $\theta$, mass parameter $\mu$, timestep $\Delta t$, dispersion branch,
or error definition. It is not reproducible from the manuscript as written.
The verifier's own trial implementations give different numbers, and its
arcsine clipping (`min(x, 1.0)`) converts out-of-domain (unstable) modes into
spurious zero-error entries at the zone edge — that comparison is invalid.

**Required:** publish $\theta, \mu, \Delta t$, branch, norm, and error metric
with an executable table generator; treat $x > 1$ as unstable/out-of-domain,
never clipped.
